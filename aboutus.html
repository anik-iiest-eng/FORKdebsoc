Why DebSoc?

The Debating Society of IIEST, Shibpur, or DebSoc, is a student-led community built around conversation, critical thinking, and public speaking. Since its early years, the society has served as a space where students come together to debate ideas, challenge perspectives, and learn to express themselves clearly and confidently.

What began as small weekly discussions gradually evolved into one of the institute’s most active platforms for debate and Model United Nations. Over the years, DebSoc has hosted Oxford-style debates, parliamentary formats, public speaking sessions, memorial debates, and MUN conferences, while representing IIEST at institutions across the country, including IIT Kharagpur, IISER Kolkata, Jadavpur University, and IIT Guwahati. Members have earned recognition in diplomacy, public speaking, and delegation performance.

But DebSoc has never been defined only by awards. Many members join with little or no experience in debating or public speaking. Through regular sessions, practice debates, workshops, and peer mentorship, students gradually develop confidence in speaking, thinking critically, and listening carefully.

The society hosts flagship events throughout the year, including BECON (Bengal Engineering Conclave of Nations), the Paramesh Ranjan Dhar Memorial Debate, Disputation, Voices in Focus, and collaborative events under Instruo and TechManthan. Each reflects the same spirit: open discussion, intellectual curiosity, and meaningful dissent.

Even during the pandemic, DebSoc continued conducting online debates, training sessions, and inductions, proving that the community extended far beyond physical rooms or auditoriums. That continuity remains one of its defining strengths.

Today, DebSoc brings together students from different disciplines and backgrounds who share an interest in ideas, dialogue, and expression. Whether someone joins to compete nationally, improve communication skills, explore international relations, or become more confident speaking in public, the society offers a space to grow.

To the Batch of 2030

If you are considering joining DebSoc, there is something important to know early: you do not need to arrive polished, confident, or impossibly articulate. Nobody walks into a room at eighteen speaking like a diplomat who swallowed three newspapers and a law textbook. Most people begin awkwardly. They interrupt too early, forget points halfway through, panic during rebuttals, or discover that “winning arguments” and “communicating clearly” are very different skills. That is normal.

DebSoc has always been built around people learning publicly. Members have come from every background: students who loved politics, students terrified of public speaking, people who joined for MUNs, and people who stayed for the discussions after sessions ended. Some went on to win competitions. Some became more thoughtful, more confident, and more capable of expressing themselves in classrooms, interviews, meetings, and life beyond college. Both matter equally.

What survives in a society is rarely just trophies. It is culture: seniors staying back to help juniors prepare speeches, people showing up after bad performances, disagreements remaining respectful, and curiosity being encouraged instead of mocked. Across uneven years and changing batches, DebSoc has managed to preserve that spirit remarkably well. Which is mildly miraculous by student-organisation standards. Most college groups collapse the moment Google Drive passwords are lost.

You will debate serious topics here and occasionally ridiculous ones. You will probably lose arguments you were convinced you would win. You may discover that listening carefully is harder than speaking loudly. You will also find people willing to help you improve if you are willing to put in the effort consistently.

And that is really the only expectation: show up. Speak. Listen. Learn. Return the next week and do it again.

Because in the end, DebSoc is not defined only by competitions, events, or committee names. It is defined by generations of students choosing, year after year, to think critically, speak honestly, and engage with the world a little more carefully than before.
